\section{Parametric campaign results}
\label{sec:parametric_results}

The complete 250-case parametric sweep systematically maps the full operational envelope of single-phase immersion cooling across structural open ratios ($\mathrm{OR} = 0.0\text{--}1.0$), channel Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 25\text{--}1000$), thermal dissipation loads ($P_{\mathrm{TDP}} = 300\text{--}1200\text{ W/chip}$), heat-sink fin topologies (Plate-Fin, Micro-Pin-Fin, Oblique-Fin), and dielectric coolants spanning an order of magnitude in Prandtl number (FC-40, PAO-4, EFL-1).

\subsection{Bypass flow partitioning across open ratio and Reynolds number}
\label{sec:res_bypass_split}

Figure~\ref{fig:bypass_contour_map} illustrates the fluid mass flow partitioning between the active inter-fin passages and the overhead clearance plenum as a function of the structural open ratio $\mathrm{OR} = c / (H_{\mathrm{chassis}} - H_{\mathrm{base}})$. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{../figures/fig6_bypass_flow_partitioning.png}
\caption{Bypass mass flow fraction ($\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}}$) as a function of structural open ratio ($\mathrm{OR}$) across channel Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 25\text{--}1000$) in FC-40 dielectric fluid. Solid lines with markers represent 3D CFD simulation results; dashed lines represent predictions from the derived dimensionless closure.}
\label{fig:bypass_contour_map}
\end{figure}

At $\mathrm{OR} = 0.00$ (fully shrouded, sealed top wall), the bypass fraction is physically constrained to $\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}} \le 0.8\%$, forcing $99.2\%$ of the volumetric coolant flow through the fin matrix. As the clearance gap opens from $\mathrm{OR} = 0.10$ to $0.50$, fluid diversion increases rapidly:
\begin{itemize}
\item At $\mathrm{OR} = 0.10$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ rises immediately to $14.3\%\text{--}21.3\%$.
\item At $\mathrm{OR} = 0.25$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ reaches $24.4\%\text{--}36.3\%$.
\item At $\mathrm{OR} = 0.50$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ diverts $40.0\%\text{--}58.3\%$ of the total chassis mass flow, starving the active fin channels.
\item At $\mathrm{OR} = 1.00$ (fins fully recessed), fluid bypass reaches $47.0\%\text{--}69.0\%$, with the remainder flowing along the flat substrate base.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Convective thermal resistance and chip temperature response}
\label{sec:res_thermal_resistance}

Figure~\ref{fig:rth_vs_or_re} presents the junction-to-inlet thermal resistance $R_{\mathrm{th}}$ and maximum chip junction temperatures $T_{\mathrm{chip,max}}$ across the parameter grid. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{../figures/fig7_thermal_resistance_scaling.png}
\caption{Total thermal resistance ($R_{\mathrm{th}}$) as a function of structural open ratio ($\mathrm{OR}$) across Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 25\text{--}1000$) for the baseline plate-fin immersion chassis at $700$~W/chip in FC-40.}
\label{fig:rth_vs_or_re}
\end{figure}

For the baseline plate-fin geometry in FC-40 at $700\text{ W/chip}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At low Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 25\text{--}50$), opening the bypass from $\mathrm{OR} = 0.0$ to $0.50$ increases thermal resistance from $0.325\text{ K/W}$ to $0.338\text{ K/W}$, with peak chip temperatures exceeding $376^\circ\text{C}$ due to laminar flow stagnation.
\item At operational flow rates ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 250$), sealing the bypass ($\mathrm{OR} = 0.00$) reduces $R_{\mathrm{th}}$ to $0.2087\text{ K/W}$ ($T_{\mathrm{chip,max}} = 171.1^\circ\text{C}$), whereas opening to $\mathrm{OR} = 0.50$ degrades $R_{\mathrm{th}}$ to $0.2506\text{ K/W}$ ($+20.1\%$ thermal penalty).
\item At high Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} = 1000$), forced convection drives $R_{\mathrm{th}}$ down to $0.1363\text{ K/W}$ at $\mathrm{OR}=0.00$ and $0.1802\text{ K/W}$ at $\mathrm{OR}=0.50$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hydraulic pressure drop and Figure of Merit (FOM)}
\label{sec:res_hydraulics_fom}

While sealing the bypass ($\mathrm{OR} \to 0$) maximizes convective heat transfer, it incurs a non-linear hydraulic pressure penalty. The total chassis pressure drop scales as $\Delta p \sim \mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{active}}^{1.75\text{--}2.0}$.

Table~\ref{tab:fom_summary} and Figure~\ref{fig:fom_pareto} summarize the trade-off between thermal resistance reduction and pumping power consumption. The global Figure of Merit identifies an optimal operational design window at $\mathrm{OR} \le 0.15$, where $90\%$ of the maximum possible thermal benefit is realized with less than $35\%$ of the fully sealed pumping penalty.
