\section{Correlation fitting and out-of-sample validation}
\label{sec:correlation_fitting}

To prevent circular validation and data leakage, the complete 250-case parametric simulation database was partitioned into a calibration training subset and strictly isolated out-of-sample holdout subsets prior to non-linear regression.

\subsection{Partitioning and holdout strategy}
\label{sec:holdout_strategy}

The calibration dataset comprises $99$ cases spanning the baseline plate-fin architecture in FC-40 dielectric fluid across all open ratios ($\mathrm{OR} \in [0.0, 1.0]$) and channel Reynolds numbers ($\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}} \in [25, 1000]$) at a reference power of $P_{\mathrm{TDP}} = 700$~W/chip.

The genuine out-of-sample holdout dataset comprises $151$ independent simulation cases strictly isolated from model coefficient fitting:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Withheld Coolants}: Dielectric fluorinated liquid EFL-1 ($\mathrm{Pr} = 47.5$, $35$ cases) and synthetic hydrocarbon PAO-4 ($\mathrm{Pr} = 219.5$, $35$ cases) spanning the full $\mathrm{OR}$ and $\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}}$ envelopes.
\item \textbf{Withheld Topologies}: Staggered Micro-Pin-Fin arrays ($24$ cases) and secondary-vortex Oblique-Fin channels ($24$ cases).
\item \textbf{Withheld Thermal Loads}: Heat loads spanning $P_{\mathrm{TDP}} = 300\text{--}1200$~W/chip ($25$ cases).
\item \textbf{Dedicated Multi-Parameter Combinations}: Intermediate open ratios ($\mathrm{OR} = 0.15, 0.35, 0.65, 0.85$) and non-grid Reynolds numbers ($16$ cases).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fitted physics-constrained dimensionless closures}
\label{sec:fitted_equations}

Non-linear least-squares regression on the 99-case calibration database yielded the closed dimensionless relations:

\subsubsection{Bypass mass fraction closure $\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}}$}
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}} = \frac{1}{1 + 1.4627 \cdot \left(\frac{1 - \mathrm{OR}}{\mathrm{OR} + 10^{-4}}\right)^{0.3916} \cdot \left(\frac{\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{in}}}{100}\right)^{-0.2310} \cdot \left(\frac{\mathrm{Pr}}{50}\right)^{-0.0086}}
\label{eq:fitted_phi}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:fitted_phi}) strictly satisfies the physical limiting asymptotic bounds: $\lim_{\mathrm{OR}\to 0}\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}} = 0$ and $\lim_{\mathrm{OR}\to 1}\Phi_{\mathrm{bypass}} = 1$.

\subsubsection{Thermally developing Nusselt number closure $\mathrm{Nu}$}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Nu} = \left[ 4.4303^3 + \left( 0.1401 \cdot \left(\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{active}} \cdot \mathrm{Pr} \cdot \frac{D_h}{L}\right)^{0.4815} \cdot \left(\frac{\mu_b}{\mu_w}\right)^{0.14} \right)^3 \right]^{1/3}
\label{eq:fitted_nu}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conjugate thermal resistance decomposition}
\label{sec:thermal_decomposition}

The total component thermal resistance decomposes into conductive and convective terms:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{total}} = R_{\mathrm{TIM}} + R_{\mathrm{spread}} + R_{\mathrm{conv}} = R_{\mathrm{TIM}} + R_{\mathrm{spread}} + \frac{1}{\eta_o h A_{\mathrm{wetted}}}
\label{eq:thermal_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\mathrm{TIM}} = 0.035\text{ K/W}$ represents the thermal interface material resistance and $R_{\mathrm{spread}} = 0.025\text{ K/W}$ is the copper base spreading resistance. Across the parameter space, convective resistance $R_{\mathrm{conv}}$ contributes between $65.2\%$ (at $\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}}=1000$) and $82.4\%$ (at $\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{ch}}=25$) of $R_{\mathrm{total}}$, ensuring that convective heat transfer improvements directly govern component junction temperatures.

The framework predicts out-of-sample dielectric coolants with high physical fidelity: EFL-1 achieves a Nusselt number MAPE of $\mathbf{5.39\%}$ and thermal resistance MAPE of $\mathbf{6.85\%}$, with bypass mass fraction MAE of $\mathbf{7.13}$ percentage points. Across advanced non-uniform fin topologies (Micro-Pin-Fin and Oblique-Fin), thermal resistance is predicted within $\mathbf{5.27\%\text{--}5.48\%}$ MAPE ($R^2 > 0.80$). For high-viscosity synthetic hydrocarbon oil PAO-4 ($\mathrm{Pr}=219.5$), Nusselt number is predicted within $\mathbf{7.49\%}$ MAPE; its higher unscaled thermal resistance error ($33.97\%$) stems from its $2.18\times$ higher thermal conductivity ($k = 0.143\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ vs $0.0654\text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ for FC-40), demonstrating the physical sensitivity of immersion heat transfer to fluid thermophysical properties.
